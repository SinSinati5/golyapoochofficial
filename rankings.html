HOW THE ORDER IS DECIDED

The reasoning behind the ranks.

Individual titles lead.

This evaluation prioritizes freestyle championships because their outcomes belong to individual players. Sina’s five-title record gives him a substantial lead over two titles each for Amir and Efe.

Traditional results add range.

For Amir and Efe’s title tie, Amir’s six traditional wins provide evidence of success in both formats. Efe holds the narrow freestyle points advantage, 35–33, across the same four scored games.

An editorial ranking based on the available archive.

Traditional is a team game, and lineups and individual round contributions are unavailable. The historical scores and participation lists are incomplete. Efe’s missing traditional record leaves a gap in the comparison. These ranks express a judgment about documented accomplishments.

What was added in this update?
  • Two further earlier freestyle titles for Sina, taking him from 3 to 5.
  • One further earlier freestyle title each for Efe and Amir, taking both from 1 to 2.
  • Three earlier traditional wins for Amir with scores and opponents unavailable.
  • Sina’s latest 11–9 win over Amir, assigned to traditional.

The earlier four-win hypothetical was a discussion scenario and is excluded from the archive.

Why are there separate traditional records?

The original six results supplied for each player do not form a fully mirrored series. Each list is retained as submitted, with the latest match applied to both players. The three additional Amir wins have unspecified opponents, so corresponding losses for other players cannot be inferred.

How are freestyle ties resolved?

Career titles come first. For equal title totals, points across the same four fully scored games provide a provisional ordering. Efe’s 35 points place him ahead of Amir’s 33 on the freestyle podium.
